Allergy and asthma walking routes for San Francisco

Walk the city with less of the pollen that sets you off.

Maps find the fastest walk. They don't know that the London planes on your street are your problem. Clean Route learns what you react to once, then routes around those trees and, if you have asthma, around heavy traffic too.

Who it helps, and how

For anyone with tree pollen allergies or asthma who walks in the city. The city records every street tree, but nothing about which ones trigger allergies.

  1. 1

    Say it once

    "I'm allergic to sycamore and olive, and I have asthma." Mistral saves that as a profile, including related trees: sycamore means plane trees.

  2. 2

    Elastic scores every street

    Geo aggregations add up each street's nearby trees, weighted by allergy level, trunk size and what's in season for you.

  3. 3

    Get a cleaner walk

    Dijkstra tries 12 trade-offs and keeps the shortest detour that gets most of the saving. It searches up to 30% longer than the direct walk.

Elastic does the math

Elastic isn't only storage here. It computes the score behind every street, and it proves every route.

  • Street scoringgeo_shape + terms + sum in _msearch, across 31,973 segments
  • Route proofa live aggregation sums the distinct trees around each finished route
  • Danger mapgeotile_grid + geo_centroid + sum by block
  • Place searchsearch_as_you_type, fuzzy matching and a distance boost over 57,293 places
  • Symptom searchRRF hybrid over BM25 and semantic_text
  • Live trafficcongestion is stored per street every 5 minutes

How one street gets its score

{
  "size": 0,
  "query": {"bool": {"filter": [
    {"range": {"level": {"gte": 1}}},
    {"geo_shape": {"location": {
      "shape": street_buffer_polygon,
      "relation": "intersects"}}}
  ]}},
  "aggs": {"by_group": {
    "terms": {"field": "allergen_group", "size": 25},
    "aggs": {"weight": {"sum": {"field": "weight"}}}
  }}
}

Checked independently: Elastic's route totals match a separate count we ran outside it: 436 vs 441, 93 vs 94 and 72 vs 72 trees.

Mistral fills the gap in the data

The city's data names each tree's species but says nothing about allergies. Mistral supplies that, then handles the voice and language.

Large 4

Built the allergen table: allergy level, bloom months and symptoms for 122 species, 93% of identified trees.

Voxtral

Turns speech into text for registration, so you can just say what you have.

Ministral 14B

Turns that sentence into a typed profile with structured output, including related trees.

mistral-embed

Powers "not sure which trees affect you?" through Elastic semantic_text.

The data, and its limits

What it covers

A 3 km radius around central San Francisco, about 28 km²: downtown, SoMa, the Mission, Hayes Valley and Pacific Heights. That's 60,645 of the city's 138,778 mapped tree records, or 44%.

44% covered

Sources

  • SF Street Tree Inventory (DataSF)
  • SF Air Pollutant Exposure Zone (DataSF)
  • TomTom live traffic flow
  • OpenStreetMap streets and places
  • Pollen Library published ratings

What we don't claim

  • Not live pollen. This is estimated exposure from tree records.
  • Pollen travels. Counting trees within 100 m, the saving is about 20%, not 70%.
  • Street trees only. Parks and private trees aren't in the data.
  • Congestion isn't a car count. It's speed, and our traffic pollution score is uncalibrated.
  • Not medical advice. We haven't measured symptoms.
